# Locations globe imagery

The globe uses actual branch coordinates. Every branch in the selected country has a photo card, including KP2 branches. The cards use skyline and landmark photographs of their towns and cities. KP2 London and KP2 Birmingham share their city photos with the corresponding main branches. These are city illustrations, not photographs of the church buildings. The branch directory continues to use its existing church photographs.

Countries with more than six branches use four columns of compact cards around the globe. Every card has a distinct position, and the scene grows to accommodate additional rows. Phones use a complete two-column grid with natural page scrolling, without a carousel or pagination. The rotating world overview retains its purple dotted appearance and original dimensions. The Earth texture, connected photo cards and image credits appear only after selecting a country.

## Earth

NASA Blue Marble: Next Generation, August 2004 global base map. Converted from the 5400 by 2700 JPEG to a 4096 by 2048 WebP.

- [Source and image collection](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/blue-marble-next-generation/base-map/)
- [Original JPEG](https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/esd/eo/images/bmng/bmng-base/august/world.200408.3x5400x2700.jpg)
- [NASA reuse guidance](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/faq/)
- Public asset: `https://images.kharis.org/site-images/main/56fb0dba60f8e277/globe/earth-blue-marble.webp`

NASA Black Marble 2016 provides the city lights, blended with the daylight surface in the WebGL shader. The initial country camera retains the globe horizon rather than filling the scene with a flat close-up. The world overview does not load either Earth texture.

- [Night-light source and image collection](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/earth-at-night/maps/)
- [Original 3600 by 1800 JPEG](https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/esd/eo/images/imagerecords/144000/144898/BlackMarble_2016_01deg.jpg)
